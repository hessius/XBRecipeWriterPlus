A library worth keeping

The recipe library can list your recipes and open them. It cannot help you find one, and it cannot tell you what one is for. This is the plan to fix that, drawn rather than described, so you can react to it before it is built.

16 September 2026 · Nothing here is built yet · Everything is still changeable

01

What is actually wrong

One person with a hundred recipes described the problem like this:

Finding the one I want for a particular coffee or style, finding that new recipe I just added, but also remembering what a recipe is, why it is. It makes it hard to know what to try for a new coffee.

That is three problems wearing one coat, and only one of them is search.

  • Narrowing the library to a kind of recipe. A filter problem.
  • Finding what changed recently. A sort problem. Today the list is A to Z with no alternative, so a recipe you added five minutes ago is buried in the middle of the alphabet.
  • Reading a row and knowing what it is for. Neither. A row shows a name, a dose, a ratio and a grind size, which is what the machine needs and says nothing about why the recipe exists.

What we want from you

Mostly one thing, in section 06. The rest is here so that question makes sense, and so you can tell us if something is obviously wrong before it is code.

02

What a row says about itself

A recipe has two kinds of thing to say, and they should not compete for the same line.

Evidence is worked out from your brews: an average rating, how many times, how long ago. It joins the row of numbers that is already there, so it costs no extra space.

A description is one short line you write yourself. It is the answer to "why is this recipe", and nothing automatic can produce it.

Every card is the same height

The rule is two lines of prose per card, always. Either the title uses both, or the title gets one and your description gets the other. So a description never makes a card taller, it just changes what the two lines are spent on.

The trade is that a long title next to a description will be cut short, as above. That is deliberate. A name that needs two lines and an explanation is a name doing the description's job.

Descriptions are capped at around 60 characters, with a counter while you type. A limit you can see is a limit. A limit you discover by having your words vanish is a bug.

03

One row of controls, not four

Stack a search field, a sort control, a filter row and a heading above a list and the screen is well over half chrome before a single recipe appears. On a library of a hundred that is a bad trade. On a library of ten it is absurd.

So everything lives in one row. Three controls pin to the left behind a divider and never move: search, list or shelves, and sort. Filters scroll past them.

  • Search is a single glyph until you tap it, then it opens into a field and collapses again when cleared. It is the least used of the three in a small library and the most expensive in space, so it only takes room when it is earning it.
  • The divider means something. Left of it are controls that are always there. Right of it is the filter vocabulary, which scrolls. Without it there is no way to know that half the row moves and half does not.
  • The row stays put as you scroll and gets slightly shorter. The buttons inside it never get smaller than a comfortable tap.

List or shelves is a pair, not a toggle

A single icon that switches views is ambiguous in a way that has no fix. Does a grid icon mean "you are in grid" or "tap for grid"? Users of the same app genuinely disagree. So both options are drawn and the active one lights up, and the app remembers which you used last. A ten recipe library and a hundred and eighty recipe library want different front doors.

04

Shelves

A shelf is a group of recipes. There are two kinds, and they work the same way underneath: a shelf is a question, asked fresh every time you look. Nothing is stored, so nothing can drift out of date.

Auto shelves: the app notices things

These appear on their own, from what your recipes already say. You never make one and you cannot get one wrong.

TEAXBLOOM PODSOVERFLOW PROTECTION OFFOTHER BREWERSINGLE POURMANY STAGESGRINDER OFFXBLOOM RECIPESSTRONGLONGHOTRECENTLY ADDEDFROM BREWMIND

That last one is new and worth explaining. If you import recipes from a service that mints share links, the app can now remember who shared it, so everything from one source collects itself into a shelf.

An auto shelf hides itself when it is useless: fewer than three recipes, or more than 80% of your library. A category holding almost everything is not a category.

Your shelves: you decide

You make one by picking recipes. The picker is the library screen itself, with all its filtering and searching and a tick on each row, because choosing from a hundred and eighty recipes in a plain list is not choosing.

Two consequences of the "a shelf is a question" model, worth knowing up front: a shelf cannot be empty, so you create it by choosing members rather than making an empty box first, and removing the last recipe removes the shelf, with a warning first.

Not in this round: dragging shelves into your own order. It is recorded and parked until we know whether the automatic order is good enough. Tell us if it is not.

Direction is spelled out, not an arrow

An up arrow next to "Times brewed" is genuinely ambiguous. So the axis is named plainly and the direction says what it means in words.

Sort byWhich reads
NameA to Z · Z to A
Date addedNewest · Oldest
Last brewedRecent · Longest ago
Times brewedMost · Least
RatioLow to high · High to low
RatingBest · Worst

Two of the reversals are there for a reason rather than for symmetry. Longest ago finds what you have neglected. Worst first finds what needs retuning.

A sort never hides a recipe. Sort by "Last brewed" and something you have never brewed still appears, at the bottom.

Favourites

A star, set by swiping a row or from the recipe screen, shown on the card. "Favourites first" is a switch rather than a sort option, so it works with whatever sort you chose instead of replacing it. And favourites are a shelf, like anything else.

06 · The question for you

The app has 8 coffee colours and 4 tea colours, so any approach leaning only on colour will start repeating itself quickly. That is the worry we are trying to test.

There is also a fourth answer: use two of them

Give auto shelves symbols and your shelves pour shapes. Auto shelves are a fixed set that ships with the app, so we draw those symbols once and you never pick one. Your own shelves get the shape treatment, so nothing ever needs choosing.

That also makes the picture mean something extra: a symbol says the app found this shelf, a shape says you built it.

What would help most

Not "which is prettiest". The real question is: looking at a screen of a dozen shelves, could you find the one you wanted without reading the labels? If the answer is no for all of them, that is the most useful thing you could tell us, because then the grid is not worth having and a plain list is better.

07

Rating a brew, even one we did not see

Ratings belong to a brew, not to a recipe, so that a recipe you retuned is not stuck wearing a score you gave it in March. A recipe's rating is the average of its brews, and it moves as you learn.

That creates an obvious problem: if you write cards and brew at the machine, the app never sees a brew, so you could never rate anything.

So you can rate a recipe directly, and what happens underneath is that the app records a brew it did not watch. It counts for your rating, for how many times you have brewed it and for when you last did. It counts for nothing the machine would have measured, because we did not measure it. Its record says so plainly rather than pretending.

The side effect is the good bit: if you have never brewed over Bluetooth, you get a brew history for the first time.

Five stars, whole steps. "Not rated yet" is a real state and is not the same as one star, otherwise every average would be wrong.

08

The recipe screen gets a third tab

The recipe screen has BREW and STAGES. Once a recipe can carry a description, a favourite, a rating and a history, those need somewhere to live that is not "the bottom of an existing tab".

ABOUT is not just a place to put new things. It also takes two things out of the brew tab that were never brew settings: the recipe's name and its xBloom pod ID, which currently sit below the temperature controls because there was nowhere else for them.

Tap the name to rename it

From any tab, whether you are looking at stages or brew settings. It opens a small sheet rather than editing in place, which means it also works when the header has shrunk away on scroll.

The pod section stays put

The pod ID is a lookup key, not a name, and nobody hunting for it would think to tap a title they are happy with. So it gets its own section, always in the same place, with the pod's picture and its own name.

If you rename a recipe, the pod's name is still shown with a USE THIS NAME button. Pressing it does not copy the name, it removes your override, so the title follows the pod from then on. One tap in, one tap out.

09

What this is deliberately not doing

  • No bottom tab bar and no hamburger menu. Nothing in this app hides in a drawer, and that is not changing.
  • No dragging shelves into your own order, yet. Parked until we know the automatic order is not good enough.
  • No nested shelves, no shelf sharing, no build-your-own-query shelves.
  • No big recipe photos. There is a setting to try small pod pictures on rows, off by default, and that is the whole of it.
  • Brew comparison, brew tagging and the fuller history belong to a separate piece of work. ABOUT leaves a space for them.

The one thing we most want back from you is section 06.

Everything else, tell us if something looks wrong, confusing, or like it would annoy you by the second week. Second week annoyance is the hardest thing to see from the inside and the most useful thing you can report.
